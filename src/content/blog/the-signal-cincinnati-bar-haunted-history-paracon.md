## Cincinnati's Oldest Bar Is Renting Out Its Ghosts

Arnold's Bar and Grill, at 210 E. Eighth Street in downtown Cincinnati, has been open in some form since around 1838 — first as a brothel, then a saloon, then, under Simon Arnold, the tavern that survived Prohibition by turning its second floor into a dining room while a bathtub upstairs quietly brewed gin. That bathtub is still on display, and it's also the spot staff and patrons say is the most active: years of reported bumps, knocks, clanks, and shadowy figures after closing, enough that the bar has been featured on the TV series *My Haunted Hometown*. Starting in October, Arnold's is opening the building to the public for overnight ghost hunts — eight sessions on Sundays and Mondays (October 4, 5, 11, 12, 18, 19, 25, and 26), $600 per group of up to 10 people, running through Halloween.

We like this one for the same reason we liked the Bruce Mansion reopening a few weeks back: it's a genuinely old building with a genuinely documented reputation, monetizing that reputation honestly by letting people investigate it themselves rather than just telling them what to believe about it. A bar that's been serving drinks near-continuously since before the Civil War doesn't need an invented backstory — the real one already does the work.

## A Coal Country Crew Keeps It Local

Not every paranormal group needs a TV deal to matter. Coal Country Paranormal — formerly Appalachian Ghost Tours, based in the Two Virginias region of southern West Virginia and southwest Virginia — has an eight-event fall calendar running now through late October: ghost tours and an overnight investigation at the Campbell-Flannagan-Murrell House, an investigation at Mountain Lake Lodge, a Haunt Fest at the Litz Mansion, an investigation at the Price Mansion, and even a "Paranormal Prom" at Mountain Lake Lodge on October 24. Team members Pamela Huff and James Ratliff describe their approach as methodical rather than theatrical — Ratliff called it "kind of a sitting and waiting game" that more often raises questions than answers them, and said plainly, "our heart is here, our roots are here."

This is the unglamorous middle of the field that rarely makes national news: a regional group, working regional locations, with a stated preference for patience over drama. We'd rather point at ten groups like this than one more celebrity buyer, and we'll keep doing it when we find them.

## On the Calendar: Haunted History Paracon, Today in Cobleskill, NY

If you're in New York's Capital Region, Haunted History Paracon runs today, September 19, from 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. at Empty Pockets Ranch in Cobleskill, Schoharie County — organized by the Haunted History Trail of New York State. Tickets are $20 in advance or $25 at the door. The lineup includes investigators Chris DiCesare, Jack Kenna, and Steve Kulls sharing firsthand accounts, alongside live investigations, specialty vendors, and local folklore presentations aimed at both skeptics and believers. Note for anyone planning to go: the venue is not wheelchair accessible.

We try to flag these while they're still useful information rather than after the fact — if you're close enough to make it today, now you know.

## What's in the Kit Bag: An Actual New Product, for Once

After a few weeks of naming trends instead of products, here's a real one: GhostStop has released the Rook 2, a handheld EMF and temperature meter with onboard data logging — readings save to a microSD card in CSV format for review after an investigation, and the device flags sudden environmental changes as "Spike Flags" for later analysis. It's built with some 3D-printed components in the US, runs about 11 hours on a USB-C charge, and is pitched as hardened against the common household frequencies (WiFi routers, appliances) that generate false EMF spikes on cheaper meters.

Logging your own data is a real, measurable improvement over trusting your memory of what a needle did three hours ago — that part's not hype. Whether "hardened against interference" holds up is the kind of claim we'd want to see independently tested in a real building, not just a product page, before repeating it as settled.
