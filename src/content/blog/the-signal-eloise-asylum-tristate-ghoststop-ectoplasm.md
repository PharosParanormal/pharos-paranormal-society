## Paying to Get Spooked: Inside an Asylum and a Funeral Home Now Selling Ghost Hunts

The Associated Press published a feature today on two Midwest locations that have turned their histories into paranormal tourism businesses: Eloise Asylum in Westland, Michigan, and Mortuary Manor in Toledo, Ohio, about 50 miles south.

Eloise began as a 19th-century poorhouse and grew into one of the nation's largest public health complexes by the late 1920s. Most of the sprawling campus was demolished in the 1980s, but the five-story Kay Beard Building survives, and Michighost Paranormal founder Justin Cox now leads tours through its upper floors, including the chance to climb into a bathtub once used for hydrotherapy. "Everything that you can imagine has happened here, and we've heard it all," Cox told the AP. The attraction's lead makeup artist, Lexi Carter, said taking the tour herself left her floored by the equipment readings. Not everyone is on board — the AP notes that family members of former patients have objected to turning the site into for-profit entertainment.

Mortuary Manor, the 60,000-square-foot former Urbanski Funeral Home, operated continuously from 1926 until it sold last year. Visitors can now walk through its embalming and autopsy rooms, photograph themselves on a slab in the morgue, or rent a five-room suite overnight for $200. Operations manager Cory Leibenguth lives in an upstairs apartment and points out the obvious selling point over most abandoned locations: working plumbing, HVAC, and Wi-Fi. The building will host its first wedding on Friday the 13th in November.

## A Tri-State News Station Spends October Chasing Its Own Ghosts

News 12, the regional cable news network covering the New York/New Jersey/Connecticut tri-state area, has spent this October running a "Haunted Tri-State" segment every Wednesday night. The most recent installment, published October 21, follows investigators into a former correctional facility where they reported chilling screams, banging doors, and footsteps during an overnight visit — the first half of a two-part finale. Earlier segments in the same run have profiled the historic Milleridge Inn on Long Island, where visitors report voices and shadowy figures, and the Zaffis Museum of the Paranormal, the Connecticut collection of allegedly haunted objects run by longtime investigator John Zaffis.

It's a modest, locally produced version of the same format bigger cable shows use — a news crew with a camera, a location with a reputation, and investigators willing to spend a night there — and it's worth noting as its own small category: local television stations treating the paranormal beat as recurring seasonal content, not a one-off Halloween segment.

## Gear Watch: GhostStop's Ovilus 5 Gets an Ectomobile Paint Job

Nothing genuinely new on the sensor side this week, but a real retail item worth flagging honestly as exactly what it is: a cosmetic special edition, not a technical advance. GhostStop is selling a limited "ECTOplasm" edition of its Ovilus 5 Ghost Box — the same EMF monitoring, accelerometer, and word-generating Dictionary Mode as the standard model, wrapped in a white, red, and black-and-yellow caution-striped shell inspired by the Ghostbusters' Ectomobile. It's priced at $579.95, marked down from $599.95, with a protective case thrown in while supplies last. GhostStop had previously done the same treatment to its Rook EMF Meter (styled after the Ghost Trap) and two other devices, both now sold out — the Ovilus 5 is the last ECTOplasm edition still available.
