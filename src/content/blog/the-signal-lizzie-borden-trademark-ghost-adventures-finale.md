## Still Fighting Over "Lizzie": The Borden Trademark Case Rolls Into Year Four

In August 2023, a coffee shop called Miss Lizzie's Coffee opened at 242 Second Street in Fall River, Massachusetts — right next door to the Lizzie Borden House, the bed-and-breakfast-and-museum built around the 1892 axe murders of Andrew and Abby Borden. The house's owner, US Ghost Adventures, sued the following month, arguing the cafe's name and its own hatchet logo would make customers think the two businesses were affiliated. A federal judge in Boston, Leo Sorokin, disagreed: in late October 2023 he denied US Ghost Adventures a restraining order and preliminary injunction, finding the two hatchet logos looked different enough and that "Lizzie" in the cafe's name plainly referred to the historical Lizzie Borden rather than to the house's registered mark. The First Circuit Court of Appeals affirmed that ruling in November 2024.

The case didn't end there. According to Bloomberg Law and Rhode Island's WJAR (NBC 10), Judge Sorokin ruled again in early October 2026 — this time against the coffee shop. Per WJAR, the judge found Miss Lizzie's Coffee LLC had been "recalcitrant" during discovery after its attorneys withdrew from the case, and ordered the company to pay US Ghost Adventures more than $31,000 in legal fees. The coffee shop keeps its name and logo either way — that part of the dispute was settled back in 2023 and 2024 — and WJAR reports it has since relocated to a different spot in Fall River. No trial on the underlying merits appears to have happened; this is a fees order tied to how the case was litigated, not a reversal of who gets to use the name "Lizzie."

*A three-year trademark fight that ends in a fee award for bad discovery behavior, rather than a verdict on the actual trademark question, is a pretty unglamorous way for a haunted-house legal saga to grind along — but it's also the realistic shape most of these disputes take. The ghost of Lizzie Borden has outlasted plenty of legal theories about who owns her name.*

## Zak Bagans and the Ghost Adventures Crew Close Out Season 31 in Nevada

Ghost Adventures — still the highest-profile show in this genre, for better or worse — wrapped its 31st season on Discovery Channel on November 4 with an episode called "Goumond House of Grim." Per ShowbizJunkies, the premise centers on a cluster of Nevada homes with a documented history of violence that were physically relocated to a new site, with the show's framing being that whatever attached itself to the houses came along for the move. Zak Bagans, Aaron Goodwin, Jay Wasley, and Billy Tolley make up the investigating team, and the network's own promo material previews knocking sounds, an unseen touch, and something glimpsed in a kitchen.

We're not going to pretend a promotional preview for a reality show constitutes evidence of anything — it doesn't, and Discovery's marketing team isn't trying to convince us otherwise. But like it or not, Ghost Adventures remains the show most people's mental image of "ghost hunting" is built on, and a season closing out is a reasonable marker of where the genre's biggest platform is pointing its cameras next: this season also covered a Utah town tied to a mass killing, a Las Vegas warehouse, an Oregon home with reported demonic activity, and a California estate with stories of spectral nuns, according to the same preview coverage.

## Gear Watch: A Ghost Box That Says It Can't Just Make Words Up

GhostStop's newest release is the Onvoy 2, a handheld device the company bills as a step up from both traditional "ghost box" radio-sweep devices and word-bank apps. According to the product listing, it works by scanning through a set of on-screen choices — letters, numbers, yes/no/maybe, emoji, or a short dictionary list — while onboard sensors watch for spikes in EMF, temperature, or vibration. When a sensor trips, the scan stops on whatever option it landed on, lights up to show which sensor fired, and logs the result with a timestamp. GhostStop's selling point is that, outside of its optional 10-word Dictionary Mode (borrowed from the company's existing Ovilus line), the device has no internal word database to draw from — so if a message spells itself out letter by letter, the company argues, it wasn't pulled from a pre-loaded list. It's built in-house at GhostStop's Lynchburg, Virginia shop and lists for $399.95, marked down from $459.95.

We couldn't find a confirmed release date for the Onvoy 2 anywhere on GhostStop's site — the original Onvoy came out back in 2022, and reviews specific to this second version only go back a few weeks — so we're flagging it as a current listing rather than breaking news, same as we do with any gear item lacking a clear launch date.
