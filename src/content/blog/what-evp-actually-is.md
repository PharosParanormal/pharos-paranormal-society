Electronic voice phenomena, or EVP, refers to sounds found on audio recordings that resemble speech but weren't audible at the time of recording. It's one of the most talked-about — and most misunderstood — tools in paranormal investigation.

## How we record it

Every EVP session uses two independent recorders running simultaneously, in a room confirmed quiet beforehand. We log ambient noise sources in advance: HVAC systems, nearby roads, plumbing, even other team members' footsteps. If a sound only shows up on one recorder, it doesn't make the cut.

## How we review it

Anything flagged gets reviewed independently by at least two team members before it's discussed as a group. We're specifically listening for whether a "voice" could be pareidolia — the brain's tendency to find patterns, including words, in random noise. A lot of what gets called EVP online doesn't survive this step, and that's fine. We'd rather have fewer clips we trust than a long list we don't.

## What it can and can't tell us

Even a clear EVP capture isn't proof on its own. We treat it as one data point alongside everything else gathered during an investigation — historical research, EMF and thermal readings, and the client's own account. The goal was never to manufacture a dramatic clip. It's to find out what's actually going on.
